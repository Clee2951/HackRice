Ohm's Law and Simple Circuits

Voltage is the electrical potential difference between two points, measured in volts.
Current is the rate of flow of electric charge, measured in amperes.
Resistance measures opposition to current, measured in ohms.

For an ohmic resistor held at constant temperature, voltage and current obey V = I R.
If a 6 ohm resistor carries 2 amperes, the voltage across it is 12 volts.
When voltage is held constant, doubling resistance halves current.
Ohm's law with a constant resistance does not describe all devices or all conditions.
For example, a diode has a nonlinear current-voltage relationship.

For resistors in series, resistances add and the same current passes through each resistor.
For resistors in parallel, the voltage across each branch is the same and branch currents add.
